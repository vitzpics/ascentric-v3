When the organization outgrows its habits

Organizations change faster than the way people work together.

The visible problem may look like execution, leadership, or retention. Underneath, the human system often hasn’t caught up with what the organization now requires.

  • Decisions keep getting revisited.
  • Roles and authority become blurry.
  • Candor declines.
  • Founders become bottlenecks.
  • The strategy changes. Behavior doesn’t.
  • Strong people start working around one another.

What worked at 20 people stops working at 80.

A through-line, not a résumé

Twenty years studying what makes groups capable.

What allows capable individuals to become a group that can navigate difficult things together? That question runs through Adam Aronovitz’s work.

It began in Boston classrooms and continued through cross-cultural and experiential learning in more than 30 countries. In unfamiliar settings, trust, authority, communication, and shared responsibility were immediately visible.

Then came building and leading organizations. Through ventures including Global Routes and The Cookbook Project, and a major merger and transition at Carpe Diem Education Group, Adam experienced the demands of growth from the inside.

Today, he brings that founder and operator experience to strategic facilitation, culture work, and executive coaching. Training through the Berkeley Executive Coaching Institute and certification in EQ-i 2.0 add structure to a question he has been exploring for more than two decades.
