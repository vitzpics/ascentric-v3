The Bridge

Culture is the bridge between
founder vision and collective behavior.

Culture Cartography maps the gap between what a team says it values and how people actually communicate, decide, trust, repair, and lead under pressure.

It helps founders, executives, and leadership teams see the patterns shaping performance, then translate those patterns into clearer agreements, healthier cadences, and better ways of working together.

The output isn't a values deck. It's a real diagnostic — and a practical strategy for what to do with what it finds.

What a Culture Cartography engagement produces

  • — A named map of what's actually happening beneath the surface
  • — Shared language for communication patterns, trust gaps, and decision friction
  • — A clear debrief with leadership that shifts the conversation
  • — A strategic architecture for what to change and how
  • — Practical levers — structural, relational, narrative — not just reflection

The Framework

Culture operates in three dimensions simultaneously.

Most culture work only addresses the surface. Culture Cartography maps all three layers — what organizations say, how they act, and who they're becoming.

2D

Surface Culture

What we say — values statements, norms, stated commitments, the language of how "we do things here." This is the declared culture. It's where most organizations live and stop.

3D

Behavioral Culture

How we actually act — what happens in rooms, decisions under pressure, conflict patterns, who gets heard, and how trust is actually earned or eroded. The real culture.

4D

Identity Culture

Who we're becoming — the trajectory, the narrative arc, the legacy being built. The culture that isn't here yet but is either being actively designed or passively inherited.

Core Principle

"Culture is not what a team says it values.
Culture is what people repeatedly practice under pressure."

— Adam Aronovitz

Diagnostic Domains

What Culture Cartography actually maps.

Structural

How decisions are made

Who actually holds decision rights. Where bottlenecks form. How role clarity and ownership work in practice — and whether leadership is concentrated in one person or genuinely shared.

Relational

Trust and psychological safety

Where trust is held, built, or absent. The level of psychological safety — whether people can tell the truth early enough for it to matter. How conflict gets handled and whether repair actually happens after it.

Narrative

The stories we carry

The unofficial narratives about who we are and what's possible. The gap between stated values and what the team actually rewards, tolerates, and practices day to day. Most culture change fails here.

Energetic

Where energy flows and drains

What activities generate momentum and what creates drag. The invisible tax that slows execution without anyone naming it.

Symbolic

Practices and norms

The recurring team practices, meeting cadences, and operating norms — formal and informal — that reinforce or quietly undermine the culture you say you want to build.

Developmental

Communication patterns and leadership growth

How information actually flows — what gets said, what gets withheld, and what happens in conversations under pressure. What the culture is teaching people about what it means to succeed here.

The Process

How a Culture Cartography engagement works.

01

Discovery & Diagnostic Design

We clarify the real question the team is trying to answer and select the right mix of tools. Not every engagement needs the same inputs — the diagnostic is designed around what the team actually needs to see.

02

Data Gathering

Inputs may include 1:1 interviews, team surveys, EQ-i 2.0 emotional intelligence assessment, meeting and cadence observation, stakeholder interviews, and communication and decision-pattern review. What people say in 1:1s versus what happens in rooms is already diagnostic data.

03

Culture Map Synthesis

Adam synthesizes the patterns into a clear map of what is working, where friction is forming, and what needs to shift. Cross-referencing across all three cultural dimensions to identify the gaps between stated and lived culture.

04

Team Debrief

Presenting the map back to the team. Creating shared language for what has been felt but not said. This session alone often shifts the conversation — because naming something precisely is the beginning of being able to change it.

05

Operating Reset

The work translates into practical agreements, decision norms, feedback practices, team cadences, and next steps. Not just insight — operational change that teams can actually carry forward.

Tools & Inputs

Diagnostic inputs may include

  • —Culture Cartography interviews and observation
  • —Team surveys or pulse checks
  • —EQ-i 2.0 emotional intelligence assessment, where appropriate
  • —Stakeholder interviews
  • —Meeting and cadence observation
  • —Decision-making and communication pattern review

EQ-i 2.0 Assessment

Adam is certified to use the EQ-i 2.0 assessment, which can be included when emotional intelligence, self-awareness, interpersonal dynamics, or leadership pressure patterns are especially relevant.

The EQ-i 2.0 is one strong tool inside the broader Culture Cartography process — included when it adds diagnostic value, not as a replacement for the full methodology.

In Practice

What this looks like in practice.

The questions stay consistent. What we look for changes with the situation.

Fast-growing startup

The team says it values transparency, but key decisions still happen privately between two founders. The espoused value is transparency — the operating pattern is centralized control. Culture Cartography would examine decision rights, communication norms, and the level of trust required to share more context earlier.

Executive team

Strong individual performers, weak collective rhythm. Meetings are polite, hard conversations happen offline, and follow-through varies. Culture Cartography would map psychological safety, communication patterns, role clarity, and team cadences.

Mission-driven organization

High commitment, low clarity. People care deeply, but decisions take too long and ownership is unclear. Culture Cartography would map values in practice, decision cadence, accountability norms, and the gap between care and coordination.
