The diagnostic

The goal is not more data.
It is a useful map.

Culture Cartography™ is a customized organizational diagnostic.

It isn’t one survey or one assessment. We combine the right ways of seeing the organization for the question at hand, integrating multiple diagnostic approaches into a picture the team can use.

Every Culture Cartography engagement is customized because every organizational question is different. We look for the patterns shaping how people communicate, decide, trust, disagree, and lead.

What we map

The formal structure.
The unwritten rules.
The patterns between them.

Structure & decisions
Roles, authority, incentives, decision rights, and where bottlenecks form.
Trust & candor
What gets said, what is withheld, how conflict is handled, and whether repair happens.
Norms & narrative
What the organization rewards, tolerates, and believes about itself.
Leadership & collaboration
How information moves, how teams work across boundaries, and what leaders repeatedly model.

The process

Map. Align. Practice.

  1. 01

    Map

    Find the pattern beneath the problem.

    Discovery helps distinguish the visible symptom from the dynamics underneath it. Interviews, observation, data, organizational materials, and selected assessments give us several ways to see the pattern. We listen to what people say, what repeatedly happens, and what people avoid saying.

  2. 02

    Align

    Make the choices the next chapter requires.

    Get specific about what must change and what needs to stop. Clarify roles and decision ownership. Reset expectations. Agree what leadership will model, which behaviors the organization will reinforce, and what it is no longer willing to tolerate.

  3. 03

    Practice

    Make the new way of working real.

    Translate choices into meetings, decision rights, conversations, feedback, accountability, and rituals. Coaching, hiring, onboarding, leadership behavior, and operating rhythms help the new way of working take hold.

A map is useful when it changes how you move.

Typical inputs

A picture built
from several angles.

  • Stakeholder and leadership interviews
  • Observation of meetings and working rhythms
  • Surveys and pulse checks
  • Organizational materials and data
  • Team diagnostics and selected validated assessments, such as EQ-i 2.0 where useful

Potential outputs

Something the team
can work with.

  • A culture or system map
  • Key friction patterns and leadership priorities
  • Decision and communication agreements
  • Organizational recommendations
  • Priority questions, recommended next moves, and a practice plan where scoped

Inputs and outputs are agreed during scoping. A diagnostic typically runs for 2–3 weeks; scope varies. See diagnostic engagements →

When it is useful

Something has changed.
The patterns haven’t.

  • Growth has outpaced roles, decisions, and communication.
  • Strong leaders aren’t functioning as a leadership team.
  • Founder or cofounder dynamics are shaping the whole organization.
  • A new strategy, merger, or transition requires different ways of working.
  • The team can feel the friction but cannot yet name it.
