The Bridge

Culture is the bridge between
founder vision and collective behavior.

A founder's instincts can carry a company early. But as the team grows, vision has to become shared language, clear agreements, decision practices, feedback rhythms, and ways of working that others can actually carry.

Most teams can feel when something is off — in how they communicate, make decisions, or hold together under pressure. They just don't have a shared language for it yet. Culture Cartography™ gives leaders that language, and a map.

The output isn't a values deck. It's a real diagnostic — and a clear strategy for what to do with what it finds.

What a Culture Cartography engagement produces

  • — A named map of what's actually happening beneath the surface
  • — Shared language for communication patterns, trust gaps, and decision friction
  • — A clear debrief with leadership that shifts the conversation
  • — A strategic architecture for what to change and how
  • — Practical levers — structural, relational, narrative — not just reflection

The Framework

Culture operates in three dimensions simultaneously.

Most culture work only addresses the surface. Culture Cartography maps all three layers — what organizations say, how they act, and who they're becoming.

2D

Surface Culture

What we say — values statements, norms, stated commitments, the language of how "we do things here." This is the declared culture. It's where most organizations live and stop.

3D

Behavioral Culture

How we actually act — what happens in rooms, decisions under pressure, conflict patterns, who gets heard, and how trust is actually earned or eroded. The real culture.

4D

Identity Culture

Who we're becoming — the trajectory, the narrative arc, the legacy being built. The culture that isn't here yet but is either being actively designed or passively inherited.

Diagnostic Domains

What Culture Cartography actually maps.

Structural

How decisions are made

Who actually holds authority. Where bottlenecks form. How consensus and conflict operate beneath the org chart.

Relational

Trust and safety terrain

Where trust is held, broken, or absent. What it costs to speak truth here. Whether psychological safety is real or performed.

Narrative

The stories we carry

The unofficial narratives about who we are, what's possible, and why things go the way they do. Most culture change fails here.

Energetic

Where energy flows and drains

What activities generate momentum and what creates drag. The invisible tax that slows execution without anyone naming it.

Symbolic

Rituals and meaning-making

The rituals, symbols, and ceremonies — formal and informal — that reinforce or undermine the culture you say you want to build.

Developmental

Who is growing and who isn't

Where the growth edge of leadership actually sits. What the culture is teaching people about what it means to succeed here.

The Process

How a Culture Cartography engagement works.

01

Discovery & Interviews

Individual conversations with key team members to surface the terrain beneath the official narrative. What people say in 1:1s versus what they say in rooms is the first diagnostic data.

02

Culture Map Synthesis

Cross-referencing diagnostic data across the three cultural dimensions to produce a coherent picture of what's actually happening. Identifying the gaps between stated and lived culture.

03

Debrief & Naming

Presenting the map back to leadership. Creating shared language for what's been seen. This session alone often shifts the conversation — because naming something precisely is the beginning of being able to change it.

04

Strategy & Architecture

Translating the map into a strategic plan. Identifying the specific levers — structural, relational, narrative, ritual — that will shift the culture from where it is to where it needs to go.
